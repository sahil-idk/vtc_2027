Gate 1 field report · Berlin V2X cellular dataset

Completeness lied.
Coverage tells the truth.

A controlled experiment built in response to peer review — testing whether "how much data you have" actually predicts digital-twin fidelity. It doesn't. What matters is which physical conditions the data actually spans.

01 · The challenge

A reviewer asked for the experiment we hadn't run

The original manuscript claimed a 16-percentage-point SUMO accuracy gap between a "complete" device (PC1) and three "incomplete" devices (PC2–PC4), attributed entirely to data completeness. Reviewer 2 wasn't convinced:

"PC1 and PC2–PC4 correspond to different vehicles, which may have traversed different routes, environments, and signal conditions. To isolate completeness as the sole cause, a controlled experiment is needed — for example, artificially sub-sampling PC1 to match the completeness level of PC2–PC4 and observing the resulting accuracy drop." — Reviewer 2, ITSC 2026 review

This report is that experiment — and two more it led to.

02 · Experiment 1

Degrading PC1's own completeness to PC4's level

Same vehicle. Same route. Same physical conditions in both arms — the vehicle/route confound is eliminated by construction. A fixed 30% test set (11,540 rows) is scored identically in every condition. The model: a transparent 2-parameter log-distance fit per frequency band, predicting RSRP from real distance-to-serving-tower (geolocated via OpenCelliD).

Result: % of predictions within 6 dB of measured RSRP

FULL (99.95% complete)
44.19%
DEGRADED (79.04%, matched to PC4)
44.13%

5 random trials, ±0.02pp. A further sweep down to 2% of the training pool (533 rows) still held ~44% flat.

Finding Gap: 0.06 percentage points.

Removing a fifth of the training data — even down to 2% of it — barely moves accuracy. Row quantity does not explain the original 16pp gap. It was almost certainly the vehicle/route confound the reviewer suspected.

03 · A closer look at the mechanism

PC4 didn't lose random rows — it lost a route segment

Before retiring completeness entirely, we checked how PC4 actually lost its data. Two structural checks, run on the real (not synthetic) missingness pattern:

CheckResult
Co-missingnessCell Identity, RSRP, RSRQ, RSSI, SNR all go missing together (98.6% agreement) — whole-measurement dropout, not a single blank field
Run-length (time-ordered)11 distinct gap events total. Median length 9 rows. Longest: 9,045 consecutive rows.

That's the signature of an equipment or connectivity outage — a tunnel, a dead zone, a logging failure — not thousands of independent random drops. Our Experiment 1 test (random single-row removal) tested the wrong failure mode entirely.

04 · Experiment 2

Testing the right failure mode: losing a whole regime, not random rows

Same PC1 setup, same fixed test set — but this time removing one contiguous, distance-sorted 21% block of the training pool, in three positions.

Finding Losing the cell-edge regime costs ~1.5pp. Losing the same amount at random costs ~0pp.

Small in magnitude, but real and repeatable. Which conditions go missing matters. How much data is missing, by itself, mostly doesn't.

05 · The replacement metric

The coverage score

Two entropy-based sub-scores, computed per device, that measure whether the data spans the range of conditions a twin needs — not how many rows exist.

  • Distance-bin coverage — Shannon entropy of row-counts across 8 distance-to-tower bins (0–30m … 1000m+), normalized to 1.0 = perfectly even spread.
  • Band-diversity coverage — same entropy calculation over the frequency bands actually observed.

Finding PC1 — ranked "best" at 99.95% completeness — has the worst band coverage in the dataset.

Both Operator 1 devices (pc1, pc4) see almost exclusively one band. Operator 2's devices (pc2, pc3) see a genuine multi-band mix. PC1 also has essentially zero far-distance samples — 2 rows beyond 1km, out of 38,540. A completeness metric is structurally blind to both facts.

06 · What changes in Gate 1

The redesign

Old Gate 1New Gate 1
Completeness % as primary readiness signalDemoted to a secondary diagnostic only
(no coverage concept)Feature-space coverage score — primary axis
(no missingness-mechanism check)Structural missingness check — co-missingness + run-length, flags outage-style gaps distinctly from noise
Value / representation checks (V1, V2, V3)Unchanged — independently justified, untouched by this analysis

Open question

The 50/50 weighting between distance-coverage and band-diversity above is a placeholder, not a fitted result. The principled next step: correlate each sub-score against actual Gate 2 (Sionna RT) fidelity across enough slices to let the weights be derived from evidence — the same "Gate 2 calibrates Gate 1" idea from the original two-gate architecture, now with a concrete metric to calibrate.
